Case Study · Recruiting App

Back to work, no reapplying

How an app for returning workers cut recruiting effort.

Client

  • Temporary staffing agency

Users

  • Temporary workers
  • Recruiting team

Role

  • UX Designer

Goal

  • Make recruiting more efficient
  • Lower hiring costs

    The brief

    The agency had introduced a new internal system connecting its main departments. Next, the recruiting team was to be brought in, with its own ticket system.

    The constraints were tight:

    Time

    Positions often have to be filled within a day.

    Sensitive data

    We work with personal HR data.

    Fixed cycles

    An assignment lasts up to 9 months, followed by a 3-month break before someone may return.

    Before starting on the design, I took a close look at the recruiting process. Everyone went through the same path: job ad, website, form, full application process. Even people who had already worked with us.

    Insight

    That raised questions:

    • How many come back after the break?
    • How big is their share?
    • And why do they go through the same process as new applicants?
    • 60 %return to the same project after the break
    • 5 %are hired directly by the client

    What returning workers want

    We surveyed employees whose assignment was about to end.

    94 %

    would use an app to get new offers earlier.

    Survey: 47 of 50 respondents

    “
    Yes, I want to have ‘early bird’ access to job offers.
    ”

    The survey also showed three wishes:

    To feel safe

    To come back easily

    To feel valued

    Instead of just connecting a ticket system, it was worth focusing on the people who come back.

    From question to idea

    In a design thinking workshop, we turned the task into a question and developed the idea from it.

    How might we …?

    How might we help the recruiting team re-engage people who want to continue after the break, so that positions are filled faster and more efficiently?

    The idea

    An app that connects employees with new offers as soon as their project ends. No new application, no starting the process from scratch.

    User Flow

    The path for returning workers is short: sign in, update your status, get an offer, confirm. The heart of it is the status: employees who have already been vetted choose with one tap what comes next for them. The data goes straight into the internal system, so the recruiting team doesn’t have to enter it again.

    The biggest risk

    Last-minute cancellations

    From experience before the app, we knew that even people who signed a contract yesterday don’t always show up for work tomorrow. When someone books a spot, say, two weeks in advance, that risk grows.

    The solution in the app

    That’s why users get a reminder shortly before the project starts. This way the recruiting team sees early on whether someone has changed their mind at short notice.

    The advantage: trust

    With returning workers, the risk is lower than with new applicants anyway, because they already know the project and how it works. Staffing positions with the same people even reduces it further.

    Testing the prototype

    We tested the prototype with employees whose assignment was about to end, exactly when they start thinking about what’s next. They went through the screens on their own phones.

    We wanted to know:

    • Is every step clear?
    • Is the app easy enough that nobody gives up?
    • Would they allow notifications?

    Outcome

    Employees who have finished a project see new offers earlier and book their next assignment directly, without reapplying.

    Less recruiting effort

    The recruiting team works with the existing pool instead of searching from scratch for every position.

    Less effort for applicants

    No new form, no full application process.

    More stable staffing

    Positions go to people who already know the project and how it works.

    What I learned

    1

    Question the brief.

    The original task was a ticket system. Only looking at the existing process showed where the bigger lever was.

    2

    Change takes time.

    Teams used to fixed routines don’t switch overnight. New ways of working need support.
