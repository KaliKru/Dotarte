UX Case Study · Form & Process

Fewer detours, faster responses

How a new form cut response time by about 50 %.

Role

  • UX Design

Tasks

  • Form concept

Timeframe

  • 2 working days

Tools

  • Figma
Table of contents

    The problem

    Reports sat too long with a team that wasn’t responsible for most of them. The old form only asked for contact details, a title and a message. Everything landed in one inbox, where a single team had to read, assign and forward every report.

    Two sides suffered:

    • The intake team had to sort every report first and felt that many of them didn’t belong to it at all. It asked for a solution itself.
    • The responsible teams received reports late. Some escalated while still waiting to be forwarded.

    Who is involved: The form is used by employees of a temp agency to report complaints. The reports are handled mainly by Operations and Finance. Operations alone has around 10 managers for different projects and locations.

    The actual cause

    The problem wasn’t the form itself but the detour. The knowledge of where a report belongs lived only in the intake team’s heads. The form didn’t ask a single question that helps with assigning. So the assignment had to happen before the report is submitted.

    The intermediate step at the intake team disappears: the selected category determines which team receives the report.

    Approach

    The categories come from the teams themselves, not invented at a desk.

    1. Asked the teams: In interviews and a workshop, each team wrote down which topics reach it most often.
    2. Assigned the topics: Together we assigned each topic to the team that should handle it.
    3. Formed main categories: The topics became main categories with sub-items. Every choice leads directly to the responsible team.

    Decisions

    The teams define the topics, not me.

    They know what really comes in. Categories guessed from the outside would have produced misassigned reports again.

    A few options first, then the details.

    Whoever reports something should be able to choose quickly without knowing the organization. That’s why the form first shows only a few main categories and the matching sub-items only after the first choice (progressive disclosure). Fewer options at once mean shorter decision time (Hick’s law).

    The category decides the route.

    The assignment happens in the system on submit, no longer by hand in the intake team.

    Result

    50 %

    shorter response time to a report, compared month to month and on average. Because the reports ran through the system, it was possible to measure exactly when a report came in and when the responsible team responded.

    A surprise: The intake team was relieved less than hoped. It had assumed that it forwards many reports. But the data showed: around 60 % of the reports actually belong to this team. Still, it now works faster, because the reports arrive filtered and categorized and are easy to prioritize.

    What I take away from this

    • Often the problem isn’t in the interface but in the process behind it. A form can take over an entire work step if it asks the right question at the right time.
    • How a team perceives its own work is an assumption. Only the data showed that the gain wasn’t in forwarding but in prioritizing.
